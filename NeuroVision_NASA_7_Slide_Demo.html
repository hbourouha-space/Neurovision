01 · Research motivation

Why study vision in space?

Spaceflight-associated ocular changes raise important questions about the eye, fluid behavior, and tissue mechanics in altered environments.

NeuroVision brings these questions into one exploratory platform, using engineering concepts to help organize observations and frame future research.

Long-term research direction: support better understanding of optic nerve health, protection, and the scientific questions that future regeneration research must address.

NeuroVision03 / 07
03 · Engineering approach

Exploring fluid dynamics and mechanics

CFD concept

A simplified rigid circular-tube, steady laminar-flow approximation illustrates a fluid-dynamics workflow.

FEA concept

A one-dimensional, homogeneous, linear-elastic proxy illustrates a basic mechanical-response workflow.

These are educational engineering proxies—not anatomical simulations, validated optic-nerve models, or clinical predictions.

04 · NASA research context

Building a path toward NASA data

NASA’s Open Science Data Repository (OSDR) and Life Sciences Data Archive (LSDA) are identified as potential sources for future research-data discovery.

NASA OSDR+NASA LSDA→Future data review

Candidate ocular and spaceflight-related study records were identified for follow-up. Their datasets have not yet been integrated into this prototype.

NeuroVision05 / 07
05 · Scientific boundaries

What the prototype does—and does not do

Demonstrates

Observation organization, synthetic example trends, and simplified engineering-model workflows.

Does not claim

Clinical validation, diagnosis, flight validation, anatomical CFD/FEA, or integration with NASA systems or medical devices.

Manual entries are user-entered and session-only. Relationship Explorer uses synthetic demonstration records.

NeuroVision06 / 07
06 · Intended impact and next steps

From exploration to better research questions

NeuroVision is an early-stage platform intended to connect multidisciplinary research ideas and make assumptions visible.

Review NASA datasets→Validate models→Develop research tools

Future work includes reviewing and integrating suitable public datasets, improving model geometry and assumptions, and collaborating with domain experts.

Core aim: contribute responsibly to research on spaceflight vision effects and, over the longer term, optic nerve protection and regeneration.
